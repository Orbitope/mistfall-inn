# The Mistfall, and the Road It Sits On

Read-only canon. The editor surfaces these files in the lore panel; nothing here
is loaded by the runtime. Entities point at it with `loreRef`.

## The Kelder road

A single cart road over a single stone bridge. When the Kelder rises — three or
four nights a year — the ford is impassable in both directions and whoever is
between the tollhouse and the county line stops where they are until it drops.
The Mistfall is the only roof on that stretch.

This is the whole reason the story works: the storm is not atmosphere, it is the
locked door. Everybody who could have done it is still in the building, and the
militia arrives at first light whether or not anyone has an answer.

## The Crown Assessment

A valuation office, not a police one. Assessors ride a circuit, price what they
find, and write it in a ledger that is treated as fact by every court in the
county. They are not liked. They are also not usually murdered — the ledger goes
to Kelder with or without the man carrying it, which is a thing at least one
person at the Mistfall did not think through.

Halloran Vane had been on this circuit eleven years. He was not corrupt. That is
worth stating plainly, because it is the reason there is no comfortable answer:
he was doing his job accurately, and doing it accurately was going to destroy
three separate people who were all in the room.

## The night itself

- **Seven.** Vane takes the corner table and opens the ledger.
- **Nine.** Bragg pours the mulled wine and carries it over. It then sits at his
  elbow, in an open room, for roughly two hours.
- **Some time after.** The yard door opens and closes twice. Calloway hears it
  from upstairs and assumes it is the assessment coming back for her wheels.
- **Before four.** Vane dies at the table.
- **Four.** Bragg finds him and wakes the clerk.
- **First light.** The rain stops and four riders come up the ford road.

## What is actually true

Aldous Wren poisoned the wine with foxglove from his own case, then went out
across the yard in the rain and forced the torn ledger page down between two
boards of Maud Calloway's cart.

He did not tear the page out to hide a murder. He tore it out first, and then
found that tearing it out was not enough, because Vane had written it and Vane
remembered it. The murder is downstream of the page, not the other way round.

The page records eleven shillings paid to a clerk of the assessment for figures,
on the twelfth of the month, with the clerk named. One transaction, once,
because he had not eaten. It would have finished him in the only trade he had.

**Bragg** and **Calloway** are innocent and both have better motives than he
does. That is the design: the two loud motives are real, documented, and wrong,
and the quiet one is a man who has been sitting on a step all night composing how
it sounds.
